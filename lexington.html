Life in Lexington

A city of 320,000 on an eroded dome of limestone, twenty minutes from open country and about a third the rent of the coasts.

Named one of the Best Small Cities in the U.S. in Condé Nast Traveler’s 2025 Readers’ Choice Awards.

Underfoot

The pasture, the horse farms, and the distilleries all follow from the same rock. Lexington sits on an eroded dome of limestone, unusually pure and rich in phosphate. The ice sheets that flattened the land to the north stopped sixty miles short of here, so weathering has run uninterrupted for millions of years, leaving deep soil high in calcium and phosphorus. Water moving through that limestone picks up the calcium and leaves iron behind, which is why the distilleries are where they are.

The soil grew the pasture, the pasture drew the horse farms, and two centuries of breeders, trainers, and veterinarians built everything after that. Farmland this good was worth protecting, so the city stayed compact instead of sprawling. Twenty minutes south, the Kentucky River has cut three hundred feet down through all of it, exposing the Palisades: rock laid down 450 million years ago, and the best afternoon hike near the lab.

Food

Nearly everything a kitchen needs is within an hour's drive, so the restaurants here were farm-to-table before the phrase existed and never had to say so. Ouita Michel's kitchens have collected James Beard nominations for two decades. The Distillery District has grown up around the old James E. Pepper bourbon works into a dense strip of breweries and kitchens. And the West African, Ethiopian, and Sudanese restaurants along Southland and Richmond Road are usually where you should actually be eating.

Art and music

Since 2011 the PRHBTN festival has put more than forty murals on downtown building faces, most by artists brought in from outside the country, and the fifteenth edition ran this past October. 21c shows contemporary work for free in a converted bank on Main Street, and Institute 193 has run a small, serious gallery on North Limestone since 2009. The Burl, a restored train depot in the Distillery District, books roots and touring indie acts Thursday through Sunday in a room that holds about three hundred.

Outside

The compact city has a practical consequence: all four of these are half an hour or less from the bench.

Legacy Trail

Twelve paved miles from downtown out to the Kentucky Horse Park. Runnable, bikeable, flat.

Raven Run

Seven hundred acres on the Kentucky River Palisades, half an hour south, with ten miles of trail and the Ordovician cliffs from up the page in plain view.

The Arboretum

A hundred acres of the state botanical garden on UK's own campus, including a path that walks you through all seven of Kentucky's physiographic regions.

Gatton Park

Eleven acres downtown, built on a parking lot, with the creek Lexington was founded on running through the middle of it.

Room to live

A postdoc salary goes considerably further here than in Boston, the Bay, or San Diego. That is the difference between renting a room and having your own place, and between deferring a life and starting one.
